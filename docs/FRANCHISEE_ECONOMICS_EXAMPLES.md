# Franchisee Economics — Illustrative Milestones

## Purpose

This document turns the $4,995 entry proposition into a set of illustrative milestones for the franchisee dashboard and sales material.

These are planning examples, not guarantees. Actual contribution depends on SKU margins, basket size, discounts, refunds, delivery cost, payment fees, royalty, reserve settings and other operating costs.

## Core starting proposition

- Entry investment: **$4,995**
- Opening inventory cost basis: **approximately $2,000**
- Target retail value of opening inventory: **$3,300+**
- Customer delivery proposition: **free delivery**
- Optional add-ons: technology, furniture, shelving, additional stock and other approved equipment/services

The opening stock is working inventory. Its retail value must not be treated as cash profit. Investment recovery should be measured using verified contribution after the applicable costs.

## Illustrative milestone framework

| Milestone | What the franchisee sees | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Launch | $4,995 invested | Business activated and opening stock available |
| First sales | Revenue begins | Customer demand is being established |
| Stock turnover | Opening stock progressively sold | Cash is converted from inventory through sales |
| Contribution positive | Contribution accumulates | Sales are contributing after defined direct costs |
| Investment recovery | Contribution reaches $4,995 | Initial investment has been recovered on the defined model |
| Ongoing operation | Contribution above investment | Focus shifts from recovery to sustainable owner contribution |

## Why the dashboard should not use sales alone

A $1,000 sales month is not equivalent to $1,000 of owner earnings.

The calculation should retain and display the underlying inputs:

`Gross sales - discounts - refunds - COGS - delivery cost - payment fees - royalty = contribution before reserve/allocation`

Where the configured business model requires it, reserve and owner allocation are then calculated from the contribution result.

## Investment recovery

The primary recovery metric should be:

`Investment recovered % = cumulative verified contribution / initial investment × 100`

The dashboard should also display:

- Initial investment
- Cumulative contribution
- Investment recovered
- Investment recovered percentage
- Remaining unrecovered investment
- Date/time of each milestone

Do not label gross sales as investment recovered.

## Earned Hours

Actual work hours are recorded separately from storefront availability.

The platform should be able to show:

- verified work sessions
- actual work hours
- contribution generated during the period
- configurable wage-equivalent benchmark
- Earned Hours

Earned Hours is an internal business-planning metric. It is not an employment contract, wage guarantee, government-benefit calculation or legal entitlement.

## Different franchisee lifestyles

The model deliberately does not assume that every franchisee wants a full-time income.

The same platform should support people who want to operate:

- occasionally;
- part-time;
- around other commitments;
- within a deliberately limited monthly work capacity;
- or at a higher level of activity.

The software should therefore report actual performance rather than imposing a required number of hours.

## Example presentation language

> Your $4,995 startup includes approximately $2,000 of opening inventory with a target retail value of $3,300+. That gives you a revenue-generating stock base from day one. Your dashboard then tracks what happens next — sales, stock cost, free-delivery costs, fees, contribution, investment recovery and actual work performed.

## Required software behaviour

The financial engine should calculate these values from transaction-level records rather than hard-coded totals. Assumptions such as delivery cost, payment fees, royalty rules, reserve percentage, work benchmark and product margins must remain configurable.

## Important commercial safeguard

The sales system must never present these illustrative milestones as guaranteed income or a guaranteed time to recover the $4,995 investment. Actual results vary by territory, customer demand, product mix, basket size, fulfilment performance, costs and franchisee activity.
