## Current V1 direction

The first range should favour:

- packaged, shelf-stable goods;
- single-serve or immediately consumable products;
- products that are easy to pick and pack;
- products with predictable shelf life;
- products that encourage basket building;
- a mixture of high-frequency/core items and higher-margin impulse items;
- no perishables in V1.

Initial category targets:

1. Soft drinks — cans and selected 600ml bottles.
2. Energy drinks.
3. Bottled water.
4. Sports/functional drinks where economics support them.
5. Chips and savoury snacks.
6. Chocolate/confectionery.
7. Biscuits and packaged sweet snacks.
8. Nuts and snack mixes.
9. Protein/health-oriented snack bars.
10. A small number of distinctive/niche products to differentiate the range.

The first SKU list should remain deliberately compact. A practical V1 target is approximately **40–60 SKUs**, subject to the economics and supplier minimums discovered during sourcing.

## Supplier strategy

Do not attempt to buy the entire range from one supplier merely for convenience.

Use:

**Core supplier(s) → specialist supplier(s) → backup supplier(s)**

The first franchise should minimise supplier complexity while still protecting margin and availability.

Supplier selection should ultimately consider:

- verified landed cost;
- minimum order quantity;
- freight;
- delivery frequency;
- availability;
- shelf life;
- payment terms;
- returns/damaged stock policy;
- product data availability;
- GST treatment;
- reliability.

## Delivery economics — current evidence

Uber's current Australian merchant pricing page states that Marketplace pricing varies by service, with a listed 30% fee for Uber Delivery on the Marketplace and a 25% fee for Uber Delivery through its Webshop product. It also lists Uber Direct as having no Marketplace commission, with delivery charged using a fixed fee plus distance fee.

Uber Direct's current Australian page states a base fee of **$10 excluding GST per delivery plus $1.10 excluding GST per kilometre**, subject to other applicable fees.

These figures must be treated as current published reference points, not as the franchise's final contracted cost. Actual account terms and service choice must be verified before final pricing.

## Pricing implication

The free-delivery promise is a major economic constraint.

For every SKU/order model we should calculate:

`Selling price - landed product cost - platform/merchant fee - delivery cost allocated to order = contribution before other operating costs`

For a $50 basket, do not assume the customer-facing $50 is equivalent to $50 of contribution-producing revenue.

### Two delivery models should be modelled separately

#### Model A — Marketplace

Use the actual contracted Marketplace fee percentage and delivery economics.

#### Model B — Direct website + Uber Direct

Model the published $10 + $1.10/km Uber Direct reference cost and any other applicable fees, then compare it against the actual contracted alternative.

The business should select the model that produces the strongest contribution while still providing the required customer experience.

## Product selection rules

### Core lines

Prefer products customers already understand and search for.

### Margin lines

Use higher-contribution items to offset unavoidable lower-margin traffic drivers.

### Basket-builders

Select products that naturally pair together and help move baskets toward $50.

### Low-margin products

Do not automatically exclude them. A low-margin item may be strategically valuable if it drives basket size or repeat ordering. Its role must be deliberate and visible.

### Avoid initially

- perishables;
- fragile products with high breakage risk;
- bulky products with poor delivery economics;
- very low-value items that consume disproportionate picking time;
- products with uncertain legality/compliance;
- products whose supply is unreliable;
- products with insufficient margin after delivery economics.

## Food compliance evidence

FSANZ states that food labelling standards are established in the Food Standards Code and that packaged foods have applicable labelling/information requirements. Current allergen requirements require specified allergens to be declared using prescribed plain-English terminology and format. Product descriptions and claims must also be accurate and not misleading.

For V1, the safest operating model is to sell sealed, supplier-packaged products and retain supplier/label evidence rather than repacking or manufacturing products ourselves.

## Research conclusions so far

1. A compact 40–60 SKU V1 is more appropriate than a large catalogue.
2. Beverages and convenience snacks should form the core range.
3. Nuts/healthy snacks can provide differentiation and basket-building opportunities.
4. The Distributors Brisbane and Campbells are strong breadth/core candidates.
5. Kelly's is particularly relevant for beverages and now has public benchmark prices we can use while obtaining account pricing.
6. IBA, Trumps and Nut Grocer provide specialist/niche coverage.
7. Final pricing cannot responsibly be completed from public wholesale landing pages alone because several suppliers require account access or quotations for actual wholesale prices.
8. Delivery economics must be modelled separately for Marketplace and direct-order delivery.
9. The $50 free-delivery threshold remains an explicit economic test rather than an assumption that every $50 order is profitable.
10. Promotional supplier pricing should be separated from normal recurring cost in the SKU model.
